## 1. Significance inflation

**Watch:** stands as, serves as, is a testament to, plays a vital/crucial/pivotal role,
underscores its importance, reflects broader, marking a turning point, evolving landscape,
leaving an indelible mark, setting the stage for, deeply rooted

**Before:** The institute was established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution
of regional statistics.
**After:** The institute was established in 1989 to publish regional statistics.

## 2. Notability padding

**Watch:** has been featured in, independent coverage, national media outlets, active social
media presence, written by a leading expert

Replace source lists with one specific claim: what was said, where, when.

## 3. Superficial -ing analyses

**Watch:** highlighting, underscoring, emphasizing, ensuring, reflecting, symbolizing,
contributing to, fostering, showcasing, encompassing

Participle tails glued to the end of a sentence to fake depth. Delete the tail; if it carried
real information, promote it to its own sentence.

**Before:** The palette uses blue and gold, symbolizing the region's natural beauty and
reflecting the community's connection to the land.
**After:** The palette uses blue and gold. The architect chose them to reference the local
bluebonnets.

## 4. Promotional language

**Watch:** boasts, vibrant, rich (figurative), profound, nestled, in the heart of,
breathtaking, stunning, must-visit, renowned, groundbreaking, seamless, cutting-edge,
game-changing, commitment to excellence

Replace adjectives with facts. If no fact is available, delete the adjective rather than
swapping it for a milder one.

## 5. Vague attributions

**Watch:** experts argue, industry reports suggest, observers have noted, studies show,
some critics say, it is widely believed

Name the source or drop the claim. Never invent a study, a figure or a name.

## 6. Formulaic "challenges" sections

**Watch:** Despite its X, it faces several challenges / Despite these challenges / Challenges
and Future Prospects / Looking ahead

Delete the section header and keep only the concrete problems, with specifics.

## 7. AI vocabulary

delve, crucial, pivotal, key (adj), robust, seamless, leverage, harness, foster, garner,
underscore, showcase, highlight (v), enhance, align with, interplay, intricate, nuanced,
tapestry, landscape (abstract), testament, realm, myriad, plethora, holistic, comprehensive,
transformative, invaluable, vibrant, meticulous, elevate, unlock, empower, navigate
(figurative), embark, journey (figurative), ecosystem (figurative)

Cap: at most one of these per paragraph, and only where it is the precise word.

## 8. Copula avoidance

**Watch:** serves as, stands as, functions as, represents, constitutes, boasts, features,
offers

**Before:** Gallery 825 serves as the association's exhibition space and boasts 3,000 square
feet.
**After:** Gallery 825 is the association's exhibition space. It has about 3,000 square feet.

## 9. Negative parallelisms

**Watch:** It's not just X, it's Y / Not only ... but also / It's not about X. It's about Y /
Less X, more Y / X is dead. Y is the future. Also clipped tailing negations: "no guessing",
"no wasted motion", "no fluff".

One deliberate antithesis in a long piece is rhetoric. Two in a paragraph is a marker.

## 10. Rule of three

Three adjectives, three examples, three bullets, three paragraphs per section. Count the
lists; if most have three members, change some to two or four.

## 11. Elegant variation

The protagonist → the main character → the central figure → the hero. Repetition-penalty
artefact. Keep the same noun or use a pronoun.

## 12. False ranges

**Watch:** from X to Y where X and Y are not on a scale ("from the Big Bang to dark matter",
"from startups to enterprises to governments").

Rewrite as a plain list or pick the one item that matters.

## 13. Passive voice and subjectless fragments

**Before:** No configuration file needed. The results are preserved automatically.
**After:** You don't need a config file. The system keeps the results.

Passive is fine where the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant, especially in technical
and legal writing. Fix it only where the active version is clearer.

## 14. Em dash overuse

Most em dashes can become commas, periods or parentheses. At most one per paragraph, and only
as a genuine interruption.

## 15. Boldface and inline-header lists

Delete mechanical bolding of key terms in running text. Convert
"**Performance:** Load times improved." lists into prose or into plain short sentences.

## 16. Title case, emojis, curly quotes

Sentence case for headings. No emojis in headings or bullets unless the target voice uses
them. Straight quotes, consistent throughout.

## 17. Chatbot artifacts

Certainly! / Of course! / Great question! / Here is the article you requested / I hope this
helps / Let me know if you'd like me to expand / As of my last training update / While
specific details are limited / As an AI language model

## 18. Hedging, filler, generic conclusions

- could potentially possibly → may
- in order to → to
- due to the fact that → because
- at this point in time → now
- has the ability to → can
- it is important to note that → (delete)
- "The future looks bright", "exciting times ahead", "a step in the right direction" →
  delete or replace with a concrete next step.

## 19. Signposting and authority tropes

**Watch:** Let's dive in, let's explore, let's break this down, here's what you need to know,
without further ado, the real question is, at its core, fundamentally, what really matters,
the heart of the matter

Do the thing instead of announcing it.

## Quick check (30 seconds)

1. First sentence: state of the world, or actual information?
2. Last paragraph: summary with nothing new? Delete it.
3. Count vocabulary hits from section 7. More than three?
4. Count lists. Mostly threes?
5. Sentence lengths: all roughly equal?
6. Em dashes: more than one per paragraph?
7. Is there a single number, name, date or place in the text? If not, it says nothing.
